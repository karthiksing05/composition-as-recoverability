## Do these papers read parts out of the denoiser's internal activations, or judge composition only from generated samples?

### Takeaway
No paper in Favero's diffusion line decodes parts from denoiser activations. The one internal measurement is a sensitivity ratio on U-Net layers (ICML 2025, Sec. 3.4, Fig. 6, App. D.1, synthetic grammar only). It measures invariance to synonym swaps, not whether a part's identity can be read out. On text and images, every measurement is made on generated output, sometimes after passing it through an unrelated network (ResNet-18, CLIP, ConvNeXt). Of the related papers checked, only Liang et al. (2024) analyses internal denoiser representations for composition, and it does so with PCA, UMAP and dimension measures on a toy task.

### Cited Findings

**Summary table** (sources are in the entries below)

| Paper | Behaviour measure (generated output) | Activations of a separate network, on output | Denoiser's own internals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Favero et al., ICML 2025 | Rule accuracy per level, token correlations, LLaMA-2 perplexity | ResNet-18 MMD by depth (ImageNet 64) | **Yes, grammar only:** sensitivity S_{k,l}; no decoding |
| Sclocchi et al., PNAS 2025 | Forward-backward samples | ConvNeXt layers (ImageNet); supervised CNN (grammar) | None found |
| Sclocchi et al., ICLR 2025 | Token change correlations (text) | CLIP last-layer patch embeddings (ImageNet) | None found |
| Favero et al., "Bigger" 2026 | Rule errors, copies, FID, Hellinger distance | None found | None found in the text read |
| Kamb and Ganguli 2025 | r² against network output | None | Jacobian receptive-field calibration only |
| Okawa et al. 2023 | Attribute correctness | Probes and classifiers on generated images | None found |

**Favero et al., ICML 2025: the internal measure**
- Definition, as returned by the tool (Eq. 26, App. D.1): S_{k,l} = E_x[||a_k(x) - a_k(R_l x)||²] / E_{x,y}[||a_k(x) - a_k(y)||²], where a_k are pre-activations of layer k and R_l is a synonym substitution at depth l — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3). The equation rendered badly in the extraction, so check it against the PDF.
- The perturbation is to "alter the subtree generated by a given latent variable, while keeping that latent variable itself fixed" — [Sec. 3.4 / App. D.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- Interpretation: "A low value of S_{k,l} indicates that the network is invariant to synonym substitutions at depth l, implying that it has learned the corresponding compositional rule" — [App. D.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- Result: "the hidden representations of the U-Net become increasingly invariant to such perturbations" as training set size grows (Sec. 3.4). Also "deeper layers of the encoder become less sensitive to deeper synonymic transformations" and "The U-Net learns to satisfy rules at level l when it becomes insensitive to the synonyms of the variables at level l-1" (Fig. 6) — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- The Fig. 6 panels separate encoder and decoder layers per U-Net block. The tool's reading is that decoder layers regain sensitivity layer by layer as latents are expanded back into tokens — [Fig. 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3). This is a tool summary, not a verified quote.

**Favero et al., ICML 2025: the output-based measures**
- RHM (the grammar): accuracy A_l is the fraction of 1024 generated samples satisfying level-l rules (Sec. 3.2) — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- Text: Frobenius norm of token covariance at distance t in generated text, and perplexity from LLaMA-2-7B against context length (App. D.3) — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- Images: MMD between generated images and the ImageNet validation set, on ResNet-18 embeddings at several depths. "the MMD at early layers converges first, while the MMD at deeper layers converges sequentially" (Sec. 5.2, Fig. 5). The ResNet-18 is a measuring instrument and is not the denoiser — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- The clustering mechanism (Sec. 4.3, App. D.2) operates on empirical context vectors from data, not network activations — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).

**Sclocchi, Favero, Wyart, PNAS 2025**
- ImageNet: cosine similarity between ConvNeXt Base post-activations (every hidden layer, plus logits) for original and generated images, standardized on the 50,000 validation images. ConvNeXt is a separate classifier (96.9% top-5) — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16991v3).
- Grammar: samples come from belief-propagation marginals on the RHM, not a neural denoiser. Activations come from a separately trained supervised CNN: "we train a convolutional neural network with L=7 layers, matching the tree structure of the model" (L=7, s=2, v=16, m=4, n=300k, 99.2% test accuracy). Cosine similarity of its post-activations "showcase[s] the same inversion predicted by our theory" — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16991v3).

**Sclocchi, Favero, Levi, Wyart, ICLR 2025**
- Text uses discrete token identity changes. Images use "the last-layer embeddings for each patch from a CLIP ViT-B32" on a 7x7 grid (dimension 768). No denoiser hidden layers or activations are described, and "layers" in the RHM sections means levels of the generative tree — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13770v2).

**Favero, Sclocchi, Wyart, "Bigger Isn't Always Memorizing"**
- All analyses reported are on outputs and behaviour: generated samples, losses, copy rates, rule errors and the learned score's output. The tool found no hidden-layer analysis in the portion read, which was all but the last 193 characters — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16959v2).

**Closest related work**
- Kamb and Ganguli: theory and network are compared on the same initial noise, output against output. No attention or activation readout. The one internal-style measure is a Jacobian receptive-field analysis used to calibrate the locality scale — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20292).
- Okawa et al.: linear probes (shape, color, size) classify generated images, with all three required to match. CelebA uses per-attribute 3-layer CNN classifiers. The tool found no U-Net activation analysis in the part read — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09336v3).
- Bonnaire et al.: U-Net activations are not examined, and composition is not tested — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17638v2).
- Liang, Liu, Ostrow, Fiete: analyse UNet layer-4 output with PCA or UMAP, and "we have chosen not to use the output of the bottleneck layer for our study of the learned latent manifold". Participation-ratio effective dimension converged to about 7 after 200 epochs — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13256).

**Probes on internals in Favero's non-diffusion RHM papers**
- Korchinski, Favero, Wyart 2026: a linear probe reads the top-level latent from frozen final-layer representations of a stacked latent-clustering network, and a "synonym clustering score" C_l is measured on the data2vec encoder — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27734).
- Cagnetta, Favero, Sclocchi, Wyart 2025: cosine similarity of standardised last-layer representations under variable replacement versus rule replacement (Eq. 26), for last-token prediction in CNNs and transformers. Diffusion is not involved — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07070).

### Inferences
- A paper that probes internal states for parts would differ from S_{k,l} in three ways. S_{k,l} needs the known generating tree to build R_l. It tests invariance to synonyms, not recoverability of a latent. It exists only on the synthetic grammar.
- Invariance can occur without decodability, since a layer that discards the input is perfectly insensitive. The ratio's denominator guards against this partly, by normalising to variance across data, but it does not show that the latent's identity is present. This is my reasoning; the paper does not discuss it in the parts I read.
- Favero's claim that rule satisfaction follows insensitivity is the closest thing in this literature to a link between representation and behaviour. It is a correspondence across a training-size sweep, described in a figure caption. I found no statistic for it.
- ConvNeXt, CLIP and ResNet-18 measurements should count as behavioural. They describe generated images through someone else's features, so "layer" there means a feature scale of the instrument.
- Liang et al. is the nearest precedent for a study that combines internal analysis with held-out combinations. It reads geometry and dimension and does not fit a part decoder, as far as the tool's extraction shows.

### Gaps
- RHM parameters and P values for Fig. 6 did not appear in the extracted text; they may be only in the figure itself.
- The summary tool's "not found" results cover the text it read. Unread tails: 7k characters of the ICML paper, 31k of PNAS, 11k of Okawa, 102k of Bonnaire, 42k of Kamb and Ganguli, and 5.8k of Liang. These are likely mostly appendices and references, but I did not confirm that.
- Biroli et al. was read at abstract level only, so I cannot state whether it examines internals. Its stated setting is an optimally trained score function, analytically solved Gaussian mixtures and numerical checks on real datasets — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18491).

## Do they test composition on held-out combinations, held-out parts, or both?

### Takeaway
Favero's diffusion papers define no held-out split of rules or combinations. They score well-formedness of generated samples, so novelty is implicit in the sampling. Okawa et al. and Liang et al. withhold combinations explicitly, and Liang also withholds value ranges. None of the Favero line tests held-out parts.

### Cited Findings
- Favero ICML 2025: the tool found no description of a held-out test set of token combinations or production rules. Training uses P sentences sampled from the grammar, and accuracy is on 1024 generated samples checked against the rules — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- Same paper, on novelty: "Moreover, we routinely verify that the model has not simply memorized the training data" (App. C). The check itself is not described, and "novel" has no operational definition — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- "Bigger Isn't": does report a fraction of copies of the training set on the RHM, alongside the fraction of strings obeying the rules (Sec. 4.3). The tool found no separate compositional-generalization test — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16959v2).
- Cagnetta et al. 2025: test data are drawn fresh from the same RHM distribution, with no held-out split of combinations — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07070).
- Korchinski et al. 2026: no held-out-combination experiment in the portion read — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27734).
- Kamb and Ganguli: no held-out combinations. The evaluation is agreement on fixed seeds, qualitative examples and a theoretical count of possible mosaics — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20292).
- Okawa et al.: held-out combinations. Three binary concepts on 5,000 images, training classes 000, 100, 010, 001; held-out classes such as 011 and 101 at concept distance 1 and 111 at distance 2. No concept value is withheld — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09336v3).
- Liang et al.: both. Held-out combinations remove centers where mu_x and mu_y are both in [13, 19], with each value still seen alongside other values. Held-out values (App. C.3) remove a whole range of x or y, and accuracy degrades gradually as the range widens — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13256).
- Liang et al., result: the model places mu_y correctly in the vertical section and mu_x in the horizontal section but fails in the intersection, which the authors read as composing without interpolating. Adding 1D stripes gives high accuracy across test regions. Bump-plus-stripe reaches up to about 70% in test regions, and in-distribution bumps can be subsampled to 20% with similar performance — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13256).
- Jeong et al. 2026 (ICML 2026 per the arXiv listing): compositional generalization degrades as more concept combinations are held out, in text-to-image models using the MOSAIC framework. Abstract only — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00273).

### Inferences
- My calculation from the RHM definition: unambiguous rules give v·m^((s^L-1)/(s-1)) distinct strings. For v=16, m=4, s=2, L=3 that is 262,144, and P*≈4,096 is 1/64 of it. So a model scoring well at P* produces mostly strings outside its training set unless it copies, which works as an implicit held-out-combination test without a defined split. The authors do not present this count in the parts read.
- There are v·m rules per level, so P at or above v·m^(l+1) covers each rule many times over. This regime therefore does not test unseen rules or parts. The result is about learning equivalences among seen rules.

### Gaps
- I could not establish how many generated RHM strings in the ICML paper were actually absent from the training set. No such number was found in that paper.

## Favero et al., "How compositional generalization and creativity improve as diffusion models are trained": setup, definitions, order and mechanism

### Takeaway
Citation verified: Favero, Sclocchi, Cagnetta, Frossard, Wyart, ICML 2025. On a synthetic grammar, a diffusion U-Net learns lower-level rules before higher-level ones, and level-l rules need about v·m^(l+1) samples. The proposed mechanism is clustering of synonyms by context statistics. "Compositional generalization" and "creativity" are framing terms. The operational scores are rule accuracy and coherence-related curves.

### Cited Findings

**Bibliographic**
- PMLR 267:16286-16306, 2025. Favero and Sclocchi are marked as equal contributors on the author's site — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/favero25a.html); [author page](https://alesfav.github.io/publications/).
- arXiv v1 17 Feb 2025, v3 4 Jun 2025 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12089). A search summary said "ICML 2026"; this is wrong and PMLR is authoritative.

**Data and architecture**
- RHM: a PCFG with L levels, vocabulary v, branching s and m synonyms per symbol, so d = s^L. Each nonterminal has m unambiguous rules, chosen at random and frozen per instance (Sec. 2.2) — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- RHM model: discrete diffusion (D3PM, uniform transitions) with a convolutional U-Net of L resolution blocks, filter and stride s, GeLU, 8192 channels, SGD (lr 1, batch 32, momentum 0.9), early stopping, μP initialization. It predicts E[x(0)|x(t)] — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- Text: MD4 masked diffusion, 12-layer transformer (~165M parameters), OpenWebText, one epoch (~10^10 tokens), batch 64, context 1024 — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- Images: Improved DDPM U-Net with attention (~120M parameters), ImageNet 64x64, 10 epochs, batch 128, lr 10^-4, 4000 diffusion steps — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).

**Definitions**
- Generalization (framing): "the ability to understand and generate novel combinations of known components" — [intro](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- Creativity (framing, after Chomsky): "the capacity to produce an infinite number of novel and well-formed expressions from a finite set of rules" — [intro](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- Operational: A_l as above; sample complexity P* is the training size where A_L passes A* = 1/2 (Sec. 3.3) — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- "Coherence length" appears in the abstract as a concept, but the tool found no metric by that name. Its operational proxies are the token correlations, perplexity saturation length and MMD by depth — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12089); [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).

**Learning order and sample complexity**
- "the low-level rules, governing local structures, are learned first, followed by progressively higher-level rules" (Sec. 3.2); Fig. 1a says the same — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- Eq. 6: P_l = (1 - m/v^(s-1))^(-1) v m^(l+1). Local constraints need P_1 ~ vm. Total is m^(L+1) = m·d^(log m / log s), which is polynomial in d (Sec. 4.2) — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- Conflict in the extraction: P_1 ~ vm does not follow from Eq. 6 at l=1, which would give v·m². Level indexing may differ between passages. Check this in the PDF — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- Fig. 2 shows empirical P* ~ m^(L+1) for L=2. App. D.2 shows m^4 scaling for L=3 — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).

**Natural-data results**
- Text: samples at about 10^8, 10^9 and 10^10 training tokens (Fig. 4a). Generated correlations match sampling noise, 1/(v_t N^(1/2)) with v_t = 50257 and N = 2^19, up to about 7x10^7 tokens, then grow with training (Fig. 4b). "the generated sentences display longer- and longer-range correlations". Perplexity saturation length "grows with training time", with no numbers given in the text — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- Images: checkpoints at 10², 10³, 10⁴, 10⁵ steps, with MMD by ResNet-18 depth converging early layers first. No numeric MMD values are in the text — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).

**Mechanism**
- At low noise the score reduces to a function of latent variables (Sec. 4.1). Synonyms are grouped by context statistics through k-means on mean context vectors (Sec. 4.3), described in the abstract as "a process similar to the word2vec algorithm" — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).
- Link to the renormalization group: latents are built by predicting their neighbourhood, and they can change in nature across scales (Sec. 6, conclusion) — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3).

### Inferences
- "Compositional generalization" here means generating strings consistent with deeper rules. That is weak, behavioural composition in the sense of the distinction this comparison is about, with the sensitivity analysis as the one internal addition.
- The order claim has two axes: training-set size (Eq. 6, Figs. 1 and 2) and training time (Figs. 4 and 5). The theory addresses samples, and "Bigger Isn't" gives the time version, tau_l ∝ P_l*.

### Gaps
- The numeric A_l curves are figure-only and could not be extracted. RHM parameters for Figs. 1 and 2 are not in the captions as extracted.
- The tool reported that no research code link appears in the paper or on the PMLR page; only the MD4 and Improved DDPM upstream repositories are cited — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/favero25a.html).

## Favero, Sclocchi, Wyart, "Bigger Isn't Always Memorizing"

### Takeaway
The title and authors are verified. The paper shows that memorization time grows linearly with dataset size, and on the grammar it places hierarchical rule learning in competition with memorization. Below the sample complexity, deep levels look correct only because the model copies. It adds no composition test beyond rule validity.

### Cited Findings
- Venue: TMLR 2026 (Journal to Conference Certification) on the author's site. A search summary of Google Scholar said TMLR/NeurIPS 2026, and arXiv v2 (1 Sep 2025) names no venue — [author page](https://alesfav.github.io/publications/); [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16959v2).
- Data and models: iDDPM U-Net (~0.5B parameters) on CIFAR-10 subsets P in {2048, 4096, 8192, 16384}, 262,144 steps; two iDDPMs on disjoint 2,048-image CelebA subsets; Stable Diffusion v2.1 fine-tuned on 8,192 LAION-10k images; MD4 (~165M parameters) on text8 with P in {64..1024}; D3PM convolutional U-Net (8,192 channels, 0.4B-0.7B parameters) on the RHM — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16959v2).
- Law: tau_mem ∝ P (Eq. 2) — [Sec. 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16959v2).
- Metrics: image copy if distance ratio to nearest training image is below 1/3; normalized Hamming distance for text; SSCD cosine for Stable Diffusion; fraction of copies on the RHM. Generalization uses diffusion loss, FID and fraction of strings obeying rules — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16959v2).
- Numbers: copy rates at the end of training run from 1% (largest set) to 100% (smaller sets). Early stopping on 16,384 CIFAR-10 images gives FID ~5.4 at 0% copies. RHM generalizing models reach "fraction of errors <15%" for tau > tau* — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16959v2).
- Grammar, Sec. 4.3 (L=3, v=16, m=4, s=2 as the example parameters): "the sample complexity to learn all the rules of the grammar is P*≈4,096". "For P<4,096, the fraction of errors decreases only in correspondence with memorization", and "For P>4,096, instead, the fraction of errors decreases before the onset of memorization" — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16959v2).
- Grammar, Sec. 4.4 (L=5, P=1,024, P_L* ≈ 10^4): "the model never learns the rules at the deeper levels l=4, 5" and "generates data with global consistency only when it starts memorizing". Also "The diffusion model learns progressively deeper RHM rules during training" (Fig. 6) — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16959v2).
- The Sec. 4.4 regime also uses Hellinger distance between two models' outputs — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16959v2).

### Inferences
- 16·4^(3+1) = 4,096, so P*≈4,096 matches v·m^(L+1) without the (1-f)^(-1) prefactor. This is my check, assuming the example parameters above apply to that statement.
- This paper shows that rule satisfaction alone cannot certify composition at deep levels, since a memorizing model passes it. Any behavioural composition score needs a copy check, and this supports the value of an internal measure. That argument is mine, not the authors'.

### Gaps
- v, m and s for the L=5 example were not extracted.

## Sclocchi, Favero, Wyart, "A phase transition in diffusion models reveals the hierarchical nature of data" (PNAS 2025)

### Takeaway
The title and authors are verified. The forward-backward experiment noises an image to time t and denoises it. High-level content (class) changes suddenly at a critical t, while low-level features change smoothly. Probing uses ConvNeXt on images and a supervised CNN on the grammar, with exact belief-propagation denoising on the grammar.

### Cited Findings
- arXiv v1 26 Feb 2024, v3 24 Dec 2024; DOI 10.1073/pnas.2408799121, published January 2025 per a search summary. Volume and article number were not verified — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16991); [EPFL news](https://actu.epfl.ch/news/a-hidden-hierarchy-in-ai-image-generation).
- Forward-backward: "inverting the diffusion process at different times" yields samples x̂_0(t) that depend on t (Sec. 1.2). The result: "a sudden change of the image class is observed, while some lower-level features are retained" — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16991v3).
- Model: tree with branching s, depth L, alphabet v and m rules per feature, with f = m/v^(s-1). Denoising is computed with belief propagation (Sec. 3). The transition is controlled by s·f: "when s f < 1, p* = 1 and p* = 1/v are both attractive fixed points" (Sec. 4). The mean-field map p' = F(p) is Eq. 8, and Eqs. 40-42 give sf < 1 + O(1/v). The critical noise is given as a phase diagram (Fig. 6) rather than in closed form — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16991v3).
- Grammar parameters: v=32, m=8, s=2, L=10 for Figs. 5 and 7 — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16991v3).
- ImageNet: class-unconditional DDPM at 256x256, T=1000, shown at t = 0.5T, 0.6T, 0.7T, T. "Around t≈T/2, the similarity between logits exhibits a sharp drop, indicating the change in class" (Fig. 2) — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16991v3).
- Sample counts as extracted: about 10^5 ImageNet samples (Sec. 0.2) and 10,000 images, 10 per class, for the normalising maximum (Fig. 2 caption) — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16991v3).
- Conclusions mention denoising "performed by a trained U-Net" and that "the structure of the U-Net with the skip connections between the encoder and decoder parts mimics" the tree inference. This is architectural analogy, not a measurement of U-Net activations — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16991v3).

### Inferences
- The experiment concerns data structure as reconstructed by a denoiser and probes how hierarchy levels are preserved, not composition of parts into new wholes.
- The U-Net/tree analogy is an unmeasured hypothesis in this paper, and it is the one a probe-based paper could test directly.

### Gaps
- The exact article number and the PNAS copy's text. The per-layer CNN values were not extracted.

## Sclocchi, Favero, Levi, Wyart, "Probing the latent hierarchical structure of data via diffusion models" (ICLR 2025)

### Takeaway
The paper shows that changes in forward-backward output occur in correlated chunks, and that their size peaks at the transition. Chunk size is measured by a susceptibility on output tokens or on CLIP patch embeddings, confirmed on text and images. The peak supplies a data-driven readout of latent chunk scale.

### Cited Findings
- Authors verified. The site marks Sclocchi, Favero and Levi as equal contributors — [author page](https://alesfav.github.io/publications/); [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/b503810343f1c7e80fab37b400dbc7a8-Abstract-Conference.html).
- Forward-backward: "Forward-backward experiments involve inverting the diffusion process at an intermediate time t≤T" (Sec. 2.1) — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13770v2).
- Definitions: sigma_i(t) = +1 if token i changed, -1 otherwise, or ||x_{0,i} - x̂_{0,i}(t)|| for continuous embeddings (Def. 1). C_ij(t) = <sigma_i sigma_j> - <sigma_i><sigma_j> (Def. 2). chi(t) = sum_ij C_ij / sum_i C_ii (Def. 3, Eq. 3) — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13770v2).
- Theory: xi ~ |eps - eps*|^(-nu) (Eq. 6) with nu = log s / log F'_* (Eq. 54). nu≈1.78 for the Fig. 9 parameters. Transition condition: s·m·(v-1)/(v^s-1) < 1 (Eq. 28) — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13770v2).
- Numbers: RHM (v=32, m=8, s=2, L=9), eps*≈0.74, masking t*≈0.3T. Masked Diffusion Language Model with GPT-2 tokenizer on WikiText2: t*≈0.6T, peak correlation length ~7-8 tokens (N_S=300, N_T=128, N_R=50). Improved DDPM on ImageNet with CLIP tokens: t*≈0.6-0.7T (344 images, 128 trajectories each) — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13770v2).

### Inferences
- The method infers hierarchy from correlated changes in output. Although the paper reads "latent" structure, the readout is behavioural: the latents are inferred, not read from activations.

### Gaps

## Closest related work (short)

### Takeaway
Kamb and Ganguli explain creativity as locally consistent patch mosaics from locality and equivariance. Okawa et al. show composition emerging multiplicatively from attribute abilities. Biroli et al. and Bonnaire et al. address when generalization and memorization happen, not composition. Liang et al. is the closest related study for an internal-probe comparison.

### Cited Findings
- Kamb and Ganguli, "An analytic theory of creativity in convolutional diffusion models", ICML 2025 oral, PMLR 267:28795-28831 — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kamb25a.html). Theorem 4.1: at the end of sampling each pixel takes the central value of the closest local training patch. Output count grows exponentially by combination — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20292).
- Kamb and Ganguli numbers: abstract median r² is 0.95 (CIFAR10), 0.94 (FashionMNIST), 0.94 (MNIST), 0.96 (CelebA) for the top model — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20292). Body extraction gives boundary-broken ELS ResNet 0.94 (MNIST), 0.90 (FashionMNIST), 0.90 (CIFAR10), 0.96 (CelebA); UNet 0.89, 0.93, 0.90; UNet+self-attention on CIFAR10 ~0.77 versus ~0.48 for the ideal-score baseline — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20292). The two lists do not match, and the difference may be versions or which model is best per dataset.
- Okawa, Lubana, Dick, Tanaka, "Compositional Abilities Emerge Multiplicatively", NeurIPS 2023 — [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/70883). Toy model: P(n) = (1 - (1-p)^t)^n, with n atomic abilities. Shape and size learned by about epoch 4; colour bias persists until about epoch 10; underrepresented colour at distance 2 emerges at about epoch 40. 50 images per class — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09336v3). The text describes scoring as a product of probe probabilities while the appendix uses a product of indicators — [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09336v3). A later arXiv version exists (a listing shows five, latest July 2025), and I read v3.
- Biroli, Bonnaire, de Bortoli, Mézard, "Dynamical regimes of diffusion models", Nature Communications 15, 9957 (2024): speciation (coarse structure emerges, located through spectral analysis of the correlation matrix) and collapse (trajectories attracted to memorized points, estimated from excess entropy), under an optimal score — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18491).
- Bonnaire, Urfin, Biroli, Mézard, "Why Diffusion Models Don't Memorize", NeurIPS 2025 oral — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17638). CelebA 32x32 grayscale, n from 128 to 32,768. tau_gen ~100K steps, independent of n. tau_mem ∝ n, with onset at tau/n ≈ 300 and f_mem threshold k=1/3. Width scaling tau_gen ∝ W^(-1), tau_mem ∝ n·W^(-1) — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17638v2).
- Liang, Liu, Ostrow, Fiete, "How Diffusion Models Learn to Factorize and Compose" (NeurIPS 2024 per the conference site): conditional DDPM on 32x32 2D Gaussian bumps or sums of stripes, with 102,400 images in the default set — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13256); [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/95399).
- Hunt, Kamb, Ganguli (July 2026): BIRD models. A model memorizes when mutual information between restricted observations and training data exceeds the log of the number of training points; generation sits near this edge — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08041).

### Inferences
- Favero's order runs across hierarchy levels in a grammar. Okawa's order runs across attributes and is governed by data structure. Biroli's speciation is a coarse-to-fine order in sampling time, while Favero's learning order is local-to-global in training. These are different axes, and comparisons should keep them apart. Sampling and training time run in opposite directions from coarse/fine: reverse sampling fixes coarse first, while training learns local first.
- Kamb and Ganguli offer a competing account of novelty: local patch recombination, with no hierarchy. Favero's locally coherent, globally incoherent samples at small P look similar to mosaics. I found no paper that tests this link directly, and none of those read addresses it.
