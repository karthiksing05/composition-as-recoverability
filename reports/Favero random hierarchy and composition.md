# Random hierarchies offer recoverability a missing testbed

**Bottom line.** The work of Alessandro Favero (the "Favaro" in the request) gives a sample-complexity account of how networks learn hierarchical composition, and it never tests the recovery your paper defines. In the random hierarchy model (RHM), a U-Net generates rule-consistent strings at about v·m^(ℓ+1) samples per level, and the theory says it does so by merging synonymous tuples with similar contexts. That threshold rests on an assumption plus an empirical scaling match, and the only theorem on latent recovery in this line is a conditional sufficient bound for one clustering algorithm in a May 2026 preprint. No paper in this line, as far as the notes reach, reads parts from a denoiser's activations, holds out rules or symbols, or scores anything on a scale like κ; the preprint's held-out strings are the closest test. My recommendation is one cheap experiment first: measure whether an RHM network's state rises above its input, the claim your glyph networks could not establish (main.tex:371). The fit has hard limits. Rule tables have no internal structure, so a part held out from the system has nothing to generalize from, and exact synonym invariance caps recovery of the merged tuples. None of this assumes any change to the ICLR paper, and its revision closes Nov 18.

**How to read the labels.** "Stated" means the paper says it. "Inference" is a conclusion from the research notes. "Proposal" is mine, and nothing has been run. Rigor is tagged [Theorem], [Heuristic] or [Empirical]. Claims about the thesis Chapters 6–9, ICML 2025, Korchinski, the PRX equations and "Bigger" were checked against PDF text. Claims marked (tool) were read only through a summarising fetch tool: PRE 2025, Ren et al., Delrocq et al., Liu, PNAS, ICLR 2025, Kamb and Ganguli, Okawa, Liang, Bonnaire, the PRX appendices, and thesis Chapters 1–5 outside the passages quoted. Figure values marked "read off" are approximate. The PDF check withdrew these earlier claims, and none is used here: that Korchinski evaluates no new inputs, that P_1 ∼ vm conflicts with Eq. 6, that ICML mislabels P_2 and P_3, that PRX's 0.7 and 0.1 criteria are inconsistent, that a U-Net "must" have depth at least 2L (a footnote expectation), and the HTML numbering of the thesis.

## Random rule tables make hierarchy learnable, and most thresholds remain unproved

The RHM is a fixed-tree, unambiguous, non-recursive random grammar with L levels, vocabulary v per level, arity s and m rules per symbol, so d = s^L; f = m/v^(s−1) is the fraction of s-tuples that are grammatical (tool: [PRX](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.02129v5), [Korchinski](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27734#page=4)). The m tuples sharing a parent are synonyms. Favero co-authors the PRX paper, whose first author is Cagnetta. The thesis, *The Physics of Data and Tasks* (EPFL no. 11462, defended 26 Sep 2025; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06106), [EPFL](https://memento.epfl.ch/event/the-physics-of-data-and-tasks-theories-of-locali-2)), contains no PRX chapter; its Chapters 4–7 are PNAS, ICLR 2025, ICML 2025 and a workshop version of "Bigger" ([thesis p.87](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06106#page=103)). Chapter 8 composes whole tasks by adding weight vectors, a separate topic that the thesis itself sets apart from feature disentanglement ([p.132](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06106#page=148)). Its theorems sit in the kernel chapters ([pp.33–49](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06106#page=49)). Chapter 6 has one Assumption and no theorem, so cite Chapter 6's "under Assumption" form over Section 9.1's "We showed" ([p.139](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06106#page=155)).

## Creative composition means well-formed new samples, scored on outputs

ICML 2025 frames generalization as generating "novel combinations of known components" and creativity after Chomsky (tool, [intro](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12089v3)), and its operational score is A_ℓ, the fraction of 1,024 generated samples satisfying level-ℓ rules ([p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12089#page=4)). Lower-level rules arrive first. In your terms that score is behavior, the weak version (Inference, main.tex:72). The paper's memorization safeguard is one sentence with no method: "we routinely verify that the model has not simply memorized the training data" ([p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12089#page=4)). "Bigger" supplies the novelty statement ICML lacks: above P*, the model "is generating valid data that do not belong to the training set" ([p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.16959#page=8)). At L=5 and P=1,024 it "generates data with global consistency only when it starts memorizing" ([p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.16959#page=9)). Rule accuracy alone therefore certifies nothing about composition at deep levels (Inference), and the RHM copy test gives no threshold ([p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.16959#page=8)).

The only internal measure on a trained denoiser is synonym sensitivity, S_{k,ℓ} = E‖a_k(x) − a_k(R_ℓ x)‖² / E‖a_k(x) − a_k(y)‖² ([ICML App. D.1, p.17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12089#page=17)). The caption says the U-Net "learns to satisfy rules at level ℓ when it becomes insensitive to the synonyms of the variables at level ℓ − 1", and the appendix says "precisely" ([pp.16–17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12089#page=16)). No statistic supports this, only one sweep compared by eye (Inference). Invariance is graded: encoder layer 5 reaches about 0.05–0.1 for ℓ ≤ 3, and decoder layer 5 returns to about 0.5 and 0.85 (read off, [Fig. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12089#page=16)). The diffusion time, the data split for x, and Fig. 6's s, v and m go unstated. S needs the true rule tables to build R_ℓ, so it exists only on the grammar.

The thesis's claim about creativity is recombination across levels: after a class change, "low-level features from the original sample are preserved and recombined to form the new sample", "a form of combinatorial creativity" that it says Gaussian mixtures cannot explain ([pp.139–140](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06106#page=155)). Its comparison covers only Gaussian mixtures and Gaussian random fields; Kamb and Ganguli's patch mosaics, the closest rival account of novelty (tool, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20292)), appear only in related work ([p.88](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06106#page=104)).

The thesis states the mechanism and its limit in one passage: "the RHM's context-free nature reduces complex multi-body correlations among observable tokens to effective pairwise correlations involving the latent variables", which permits "clustering token groups that correspond to the same latent variable" ([p.140](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06106#page=156)). Korchinski's Lemma 1, "Synonyms have identical context vectors", proves the corresponding property ([p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27734#page=19)). The thesis lists context dependence as future work without drawing the consequence that it breaks this identity (Inference; [p.141](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06106#page=157)). Text and image evidence is correlational: token correlations, perplexity and ResNet-18 MMD by depth, all on generated output (tool, ICML Sec. 5). The forward-backward papers add a phase transition and a susceptibility peak, again measured through outputs or a separate network (tool).

The thesis also places itself away from your question. It focuses on how latent changes manifest in data, "in contrast with common approaches which attempt to reconstruct the latent representations from visible data" ([p.85](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06106#page=101)). "Linear probe" has zero hits in its 333 pages, none of its four "held-out" hits concerns parts or rules, and its four future directions include no activation readout ([pp.141–142](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06106#page=157)). Interpretability appears only as outlook. A paper that decodes latents from denoiser states, or holds out rules, is unanticipated by the thesis (Inference).

## The May 2026 preprint comes closest, and it tests new strings of training parts

Korchinski, Favero and Wyart is his most recent paper on these topics; the newest listing, on forgetting in a physics foundation model ([arXiv, Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40857)), is off-topic. It moves from diffusion to latent prediction and treats diffusion as a token-level baseline. **Held-out strings are scored:** SLC reports performance on "a held-out test set of 2000 samples", and Fig. 10 plots "Test clustering accuracy" per layer at L=7, P=9797, comparing learned hard labels with true latents under a Hungarian map ([pp.25–26](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27734#page=25)). This withdraws the earlier note that no new inputs were evaluated. **Held-out rules, tuples, symbols and grammars do not appear:** the proof requires every grammatical tuple to be observed ([p.21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27734#page=21)), and ILC takes the observed tuples as the support ([p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27734#page=4)). The theorem gives parsing of a new string by table lookup, which the authors leave unstated (Inference).

Three of its measures map onto your framing, and none reaches your definition (Inference). Fig. 10 reads the hard labels of clustering heads trained for that purpose, which your paper classes as weak decomposition (main.tex:72). The Fig. 3 and Fig. 12 linear probes target only the root, with labels the unsupervised learner lacks, and the probe trains on the pretraining set ([p.27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27734#page=27)). The closest analog of recoverability is Fig. 13 right: linear probes for h^(ℓ), ℓ = 2, 3, 4, from data2vec's teacher target, read as "all latents being linearly encoded in the teacher" ([p.27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27734#page=27)). Its random-init baseline sits near 0.75 at m=3, ℓ=2 (read off), a warning that a floor is present at small m. No figure reports an input floor or a scale like κ.

Probes and clustering scores also disagree in this paper, which matters for any joint report. Data2vec's root probes reach 1.0 on final-layer features while its last-layer clustering score is about 0.03 at m=6 (read off; Inference from [Figs. 13–14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27734#page=27)). A low synonym score at the output therefore says little about whether the latent is readable. Among neighbors, only Liang et al. analyse a denoiser's internals with held-out combinations and held-out value ranges, through PCA, UMAP and dimension measures on a toy task, and Okawa et al. withhold combinations while scoring outputs (tool: [Liang](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13256), [Okawa](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09336v3)).

## Your paper gains a ground truth and an input floor, and it meets five misfits

**What the RHM offers (Inference).** Your paper has no generative hierarchy in its data (parts are drawn independently, main.tex:141), scores only leaves in depth experiments (main.tex:271), tests no denoiser, and has no account of when training produces recoverable states. Its sharpest open problem is that "no condition we tried lifts a state above its input" (main.tex:371), with a failed calibration behind it (main.tex:689). The RHM brings known latents at every level, so z(x) exists where nouns and photographs lack one (main.tex:164), plus predicted data thresholds where your only count is d + 1 distinct parts (main.tex:613). Those two quantities differ: your count concerns spans of parts, and theirs concerns detectable correlations.

**The key bridge (Proposal).** Korchinski's parent context vector Φ_{ℓ,a} = E[e_Z | T = ν] for any ν with parent a, a continuous vector in the simplex and identical across synonyms ([p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27734#page=5); [Lemma 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27734#page=19)), can serve as the system-independent description your definition requires (main.tex:159). It is a property of the grammar, estimable from the generator alone. It turns a finite symbol into a code on which a readout can be fit on some symbols and tested on others.

**Exact invariance caps recovery of what it merged (Proposal, my derivation, unreviewed).** Rule choice is independent of everything else given the parent, so a state that depends on a tuple t only through its parent ν has best readout E[e(t) | ν], giving ε equal to the within-parent variance fraction of the code. For a code independent of the rule table this is about 1 − 1/m, and in your printed two-role form κ = 1 − 2ε (main.tex:162) it gives κ ≈ 2/m − 1. This turns the notes' observation that invariance is distinct from decodability into a bound, for exact invariance only; Fig. 6's graded values fall outside it. The paper's productive definition, which uses "the state of each composite as its part code" (main.tex:159), lets abstraction pass, while a tuple-level z(x) fails it. The declared description therefore decides whether the same U-Net counts as strongly compositional, which sharpens your framing: decomposition here returns the type (parent) and loses the token (synonym), and generation re-expands with a possibly different synonym.

| Misfit | Why it bites | Handling (Proposal) |
|---|---|---|
| Rule tables have no internal structure | A tuple's parent is independent of its symbols, apart from the without-replacement constraint; "these tuples lack internal structure, they can only be memorized" ([ICML p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12089#page=6)). A held-out tuple has no content signal, and a held-out symbol's strings are never seen | Hold parts out from the readout only (the design you use for nouns), or leave the RHM (Option 5) |
| Finite vocabulary vs continuous codes | One-hot codes make the off-pool dimensions unfittable, so ε_ref leaves 0 and κ's scale collapses; arbitrary random codes make informativeness empty | Use Φ compressed well below the pool size, since the population estimate needs "many more than d parts" (main.tex:621) |
| Lookup lemma | A table readout decodes any relation injective on a finite vocabulary (main.tex:541); unambiguity makes the RHM exactly this case | Never present Korchinski's theorem as recoverability; declare ridge readouts, and report a table baseline |
| Independent draws vs grammaticality | Your definition draws parts independently (main.tex:141), which yields grammatical tuples with probability f. At f = 1, (1−f)⁻¹ diverges and the token and latent context signals vanish ([Eq. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12089#page=7), [Assumption 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27734#page=18)) | Declare the grammar's joint law as the draw, or use well-separated positions, and measure both references empirically |
| Fixed tree, disjoint level vocabularies | Roles are positions; no part moves between levels, and no analog of a converse paraphrase exists (tool, PRX; main.tex:282) | Limit claims to positional roles |

**Option 1, recommended first (Proposal).** It tests whether a trained state holds a latent that its input does not hold linearly, using parts held out from the readout. Compute the affine readout from the whole-string one-hot input to level-ℓ latents, without training, across m, s and ℓ, and pick parameters with a low floor. Then train SLC, whose Fig. 3 cost about 10 H100-hours ([p.27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27734#page=27)), with P below and above vm³; fit ridge readouts from layers to Φ on a pool of symbols, and evaluate on other symbols in held-out strings, with sum and concatenation references and random-init controls. Reuse your pre-fixed bar: input readout at most 0.5, state at least 0.6 (main.tex:689). This separates two possibilities: states that are continuous in context statistics, which pass, and states that act as discrete clusters, which fail on held-out symbols. **Counts against it:** an input floor above 0.5 at every usable setting; trained states level with random-init, as Fig. 13's baseline warns; or high accuracy on training symbols with κ near 0 on the others, which means the RHM cannot host your definition without added structure, and the lookup lemma then voids the training-symbol result. **Paper change:** on a pass, a new subsection on hierarchical data, plus revised text at main.tex:371 and the limitations at main.tex:510. Your recorded ruling that the toy establishes dissociation only would need your explicit revisit. On a fail, at most one limitation sentence.

**Option 2 (Proposal).** It tests a conceptual claim by proof: exact synonym invariance bounds recovery of merged tuples, and the choice of z(x) sets which level a system is compositional at. It also yields a hypothesis: a low-level part's κ could rise and then fall in a layer as merging sets in, resembling Pythia-1.4b's peak and decline (main.tex:454). That link is untested and belongs nowhere in the paper until Option 3 supports it. **Counts against it:** an error in the conditional-independence step, or graded S values so far from zero that the exact bound never constrains a real network, which leaves a true and useless statement. **Paper change:** one proposition in the theory appendix, and a sentence requiring z(x) to name a level at main.tex:159. It is the only option that adds theory without new data.

**Option 3 (Proposal).** It tests ICML's unquantified caption and your inclusion hypothesis on the same denoiser: as P and training time grow, do A_ℓ on non-copies, S_{k,ℓ−1} and κ of level-(ℓ−1) latents move together? It also supplies a statistic where the source has none. **Counts against it:** A_ℓ rising on non-copies while κ stays flat would put the denoiser in your lower-left cell (main.tex:186, 496) and weaken the inclusion's substantive evidence under the declared readout class. S falling without A_ℓ rising would count against the caption. **Paper change:** the first denoiser experiment in a paper that has none, a noise-level variable it lacks entirely, and a declared copy threshold, since neither ICML nor "Bigger" supplies one for the RHM. ICML's U-Net uses 8,192 channels (tool), so run it after Option 1 and on a narrowed network if that keeps the known scaling.

**Option 4 (Proposal).** It tests composition under a controlled split, which no RHM paper has. Rule choices are independent given the parents, so withhold specific pairs of sibling rules: this is a true new-pairs test of training parts, and the grammar predicts success. Separately, withhold grammatical tuples: their symbols are training parts, yet the parent is arbitrary, like an idiom, so only context in the string can identify it, and belief propagation with known rules gives a computable ceiling. **Counts against it:** a readout above the ceiling means leakage; a readout at chance with a ceiling well above chance means the network uses no context within one composite, which limits this test's reach. **Paper change:** one design row, with explicit wording that a held-out tuple is a new pair of training parts and leaves your held-out-part criterion unmet.

**Option 5 (Proposal).** It tests recovery of parts held out from the system, the only route to your strongest design: give symbols feature vectors and make rules functions of them, interpolating from random tables to structured ones. The thesis names "random production rules" as a simplification and proposes extensions only for topology and context ([p.141](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06106#page=157)), so this is open ground. **Counts against it:** the zero-structure endpoint failing to reproduce Option 1, or held-out recovery tracking input readout throughout, which repeats main.tex:371. **Paper change:** none now; it is a follow-up paper with new theory, since every threshold above assumes random tables.

**Rejected uses (Inference).** Do not cite Korchinski's theorem as proof that parts are recoverable; S_{k,ℓ} cannot stand in for κ; and the "library of reusable parts" passage is framing ([thesis p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06106#page=30)), unusable as evidence. ## Conclusion

The two programs measure opposite sides of one operation. Favero's line studies abstraction: how much data a network needs to merge tuples into a parent and then generate from it. Your paper studies decomposition: what returns from a composite. On the RHM the conflict becomes exact, because merging tokens is what makes learning cheap in their theory and it is also what your concatenation reference penalizes. A level-indexed definition of recoverability resolves that conflict, and I expect that to be the lasting theoretical gain from this comparison, pending review of the derivation.

The practical gain depends on one number nobody has reported: how far an RHM network's state rises above the linear readout of its own input on symbols held out from the readout. A clear rise would give your paper the learned recoverability its toys could not, in a setting with a data-scaling theory attached. A failure would be informative too, because it would mean random rule tables support behavior and clustering while leaving nothing for a readout to generalize across, which would make internal structure in rules the next thing to test.
