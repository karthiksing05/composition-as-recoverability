TEST-TIME COMPOSITIONAL GENERALIZATION
IN DIFFUSION MODELS VIA PROTOTYPE DISCOVERY
Anonymous authors
Paper under double-blind review
ABSTRACT
Compositional generalization asks a model to produce what it has never seen by
recombining what it knows. Diffusion methods for this problem typically assume
that what can be reused is specified in advance, through labels, prompts, or a trained
concept library. We ask whether a pretrained diffusion model can instead discover
its own reusable prototypes. A prototype is a mode of a noisy marginal pt(xt). We
perform gradient ascent on the learned score sθ(xt, t) ≈∇xt log pt(xt) from a
datum to recover a mode and fit a local Gaussian around it, and the noise level t
sets the level of abstraction. On pretrained CIFAR-10 and CelebA-HQ DDPMs, the
prototypes recovered from different data are distinct at fine levels and merge as the
level coarsens, while a datum’s own prototype changes sharply from level to level.
A single datum therefore induces several distinct, reusable prototypes rather than
one prototype under increasing blur. With these discovered prototypes, at test time
we select those that explain a single out-of-distribution query and compose them
into a product of experts whose closed-form score guides the composable diffusion
sampler. On held-out compositions of ColorMNIST and CelebA, the samples
generated from one query generalize to other instances of the unseen class and
outperform the same sampler conditioned on trained concept tokens. A pretrained
diffusion model thus already contains prototypes at many levels of abstraction, and
they can be recovered and composed without supervision.

INTRODUCTION
Human intelligence is compositional. After learning familiar parts, relations, or words, people can
recombine them to understand and produce novel configurations. This ability has long motivated
work in cognitive science and linguistics. In these fields, systematic generalization is often linked to
reusable primitives and operators for recombination (Fodor & Pylyshyn, 1988; Lake et al., 2017).
It is also a central challenge for deep learning, where neural networks may interpolate well within
a training distribution but fail when familiar primitives appear in unfamiliar combinations (Lake
& Baroni, 2018; Ruis et al., 2020). The key question is therefore not only whether models learn
useful representations, but whether they can discover reusable units and compose them at test time.
Work in deep learning often addresses this problem by designing representations or architectures
that support composition. Neural module networks, for example, assemble learned components from
linguistic or program structure (Andreas et al., 2016b;a). Benchmarks such as SCAN test systematic
recombination (Lake & Baroni, 2018; Ruis et al., 2020). Visual generation methods likewise ask
whether learned objects, attributes, or concepts can be recombined out of distribution. Despite this
progress, these approaches often assume that the relevant primitives are supplied by the benchmark,
labels, prompts, or a predefined concept library.
A probabilistic view offers a complementary perspective. Concepts can be treated as high-probability
regions in an energy landscape, while composition requires satisfying multiple constraints at once.
This idea appears in Hopfield attractors and mixed states (Hopfield, 1982; Amit et al., 1985), product-
of-experts models (PoE) (Hinton, 2002), and recent energy-based or diffusion composition methods
(Du et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2022; Du et al., 2023). However, these methods typically assume that
the component distributions or conditioning signals are given. This leaves a gap between prototype
discovery and compositional generalization. In visual domains, the relevant primitive may depend on
the query and emerge at different levels of abstraction, such as digit identity, color, texture, or facial

Diffusion models offer a natural setting for this view. A pretrained diffusion model learns time-
indexed scores sθ(xt, t) ≈∇xt log pt(xt) for the noisy marginals pt(xt). We use those scores for
discovery rather than reverse sampling. Each noise level smooths the data distribution at a different

set by t. A single datum can therefore induce several prototypes across noise levels. Because t orders
them by scale, they form a hierarchy. Ascending the score from a datum recovers its mode at a given
level, and a local Gaussian centered at that mode describes the prototype’s extent.
We recover prototypes for held-out images across all noise levels using pretrained DDPMs for CIFAR-
10 and CelebA-HQ, and analyze their behavior. The prototypes of different data are far apart at fine
levels and merge as the level coarsens, while a datum’s own prototype changes sharply from level to

prototypes, ordered by scale into a hierarchy. We then ask whether an out-of-distribution datum can
be written as a combination of prototypes the model already has. Our method selects prototypes for
one such query and combines them into a product-of-experts distribution that drives the composable

of held-out compositions, the samples inferred from a single query generalize to other instances of
the unseen class and outperform the same sampler conditioned on trained concept tokens.

• We recover prototypes from a pretrained diffusion model without labels, a concept library, or
additional training. Each prototype is a mode of a noisy marginal pt(xt), reached by score ascent
and described by a local Gaussian, and the noise level t sets its level of abstraction.
• We show on pretrained CIFAR-10 and CelebA-HQ DDPMs that a single datum induces distinct

held-out attribute compositions, the generated samples match a near copy of the query on attribute
correctness while exceeding it in diversity, whereas the same sampler conditioned on trained
concept tokens (Liu et al., 2022) lands on neighboring classes and query editing reproduces only
the query.

RELATED WORK
Prototype discovery
Prior work on prototype discovery aims to identify reusable abstractions
between pixels and labels, such as parts, attributes, or object-level factors. It has studied such concepts
as interpretable directions in representation space (Bau et al., 2017; Kim et al., 2018; Ghorbani et al.,
2019), as discriminative prototypes (Chen et al., 2019), or as composable generative units (Du
et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2023). These methods reveal meaningful visual structure, but they are often
discriminative, rely on external supervision, or do not directly expose a hierarchy over the units they
discover.
From a statistical perspective, prototypes can be interpreted as local density modes, with examples
assigned to their basins of attraction. This connects prototype discovery to mode-seeking and hierar-
chical clustering methods such as mean-shift (Comaniciu & Meer, 2002), density-derivative-ratio
mode estimation (Sasaki et al., 2018), cluster trees (Chaudhuri & Dasgupta, 2010), and hierarchical
prototype models (Wang et al., 2025). We instead recover prototypes by seeking modes of a pretrained
diffusion model at multiple noise levels. Diffusion models admit a deep latent-variable interpretation
across noise levels (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Ho et al., 2020; Kingma et al., 2021; Sønderby et al.,
2016; Vahdat & Kautz, 2020). Recent work also shows that diffusion timesteps organize information
from coarse semantic structure to fine detail, with phase-transition behavior at intermediate noise
levels (Sclocchi et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2024). Motivated by this view, we discover prototypes

Compositional generalization
Compositional generalization is the ability to recombine familiar
primitives into novel configurations, a central problem in cognitive science, linguistics, and machine
learning (Fodor & Pylyshyn, 1988; Lake et al., 2017; Lake & Baroni, 2018). In AI, this ability has been
studied through diagnostic benchmarks such as SCAN and gSCAN (Lake & Baroni, 2018; Ruis et al.,
2020), modular architectures that compose learned neural components (Andreas et al., 2016b;a), and
data-augmentation or semantic-parsing methods that encourage systematic recombination (Andreas,
2020; Yin et al., 2021). These approaches differ in implementation, but typically share the assumption
that the relevant primitives are already specified by the benchmark, the language, or a predefined
module set.
A complementary line of work provides a probabilistic framework for composition. Product-of-
experts (PoE) models combine constraints by multiplying concept densities (Hinton, 2002). Energy-
based models use this principle to compose visual concepts (Du et al., 2020). Composable diffusion
models implement analogous composition by adding concept-specific scores (Liu et al., 2022), with
later work improving sampling through MCMC corrections (Du et al., 2023). Classifier-free guidance
provides a related score-combination mechanism for conditional generation (Ho & Salimans, 2022).
These methods give a mathematical account of how concepts can be composed, but still assume that
the component distributions or conditioning signals are available beforehand.
Our work connects prototype discovery with compositional generalization, following the view that
systematic generalization requires both reusable parts and an operator for recombining them (Fodor
& Pylyshyn, 1988; Lake et al., 2017). The work closest to our setting, Liu et al. (2023), learns a
shared library of concept embeddings from unlabeled images, whereas we discover query-specific
primitives at test time as modes of the unconditional diffusion density and compose their local
Gaussian approximations without a predefined concept vocabulary.
Diffusion models
Diffusion models learn time-indexed score fields for the noisy marginals pt(x)
and are typically used as generative samplers (Vincent, 2011; Song & Ermon, 2019; Ho et al., 2020;
Song et al., 2021). We repurpose this learned score geometry for prototype discovery. Instead
of immediately running the reverse sampler, we use sθ(x, t) ≈∇x log pt(x) to seek modes of
intermediate marginals. Recent analyses support this view by showing that diffusion timesteps
encode structure across scales. Class identity disappears sharply while lower-level features persist
across a phase transition (Sclocchi et al., 2025). Generative trajectories commit to classes at sharp
symmetry-breaking and speciation times (Raya & Ambrogioni, 2023; Biroli et al., 2024), while
intermediate marginals are well described by mixtures whose components refine as t decreases (Wang
& Vastola, 2024; Biroli et al., 2024). We measure the corresponding structure directly on the modes
of pt and use those modes as prototypes at the granularity determined by t.

PROTOTYPE DISCOVERY ACROSS DIFFUSION NOISE LEVELS
In this section, we describe how to discover prototypes from a pretrained diffusion model given
a query image, with no labels, no concept library, and no additional training. We first show that
the noisy marginals expose density modes at different scales (Section 3.1), then describe how to
recover those modes through score ascent (Section 3.2). Each recovered mode is a prototype whose
granularity is set by the noise level. Section 4 analyzes the modes found from pretrained DDPMs,
and Section 5 uses them for test-time compositional generalization.

explicit taxonomy. We recover modes of pt at several noise levels and treat them as prototypes at the
granularity set by t.

TEST-TIME COMPOSITIONAL GENERALIZATION FROM DISCOVERED
PROTOTYPES
Section 4 shows that a single datum induces distinct prototypes across noise levels, each abstracting
the datum in its own way. We now test whether these prototypes support generalization. A single
out-of-distribution query should be representable as a combination of discovered prototypes, and
the combination should capture the query’s unseen class rather than the query alone. A held-out
composition is an unseen combination of familiar attributes, so the prototypes recovered from such a
query should carry those attributes, and recombining them should yield a distribution over the whole
class. In effect, we infer an OOD class distribution from one OOD sample. Because the prototypes
are Gaussian densities, their product has a closed form, and we compare the resulting sampler against
composable diffusion driven by trained concept tokens.

5.1
FROM DISCOVERED PROTOTYPES TO A COMPOSABLE SCORE
Given one out-of-distribution query xq, we run mode ascent from xq at several noise levels, as
described in Section 3.2, and collect a pool of candidate prototypes {qj}. A greedy submodular
selection keeps the K prototypes that jointly best explain the query per dimension, and the selected
experts are combined into a weighted product of experts (Hinton, 2002). Each expert is first rescaled to
the clean variable x0 so that all experts share the random variable required by the product (Section C).
The product qT remains Gaussian with a closed-form mean and covariance, and the score of its
forward-diffused marginal is analytic at every t.
Sampling then reduces to composable diffusion (Liu et al., 2022). Here the backbone is class-
conditional, with noise prediction εθ(xt, t, c) for trained classes c ∈C and a null token ∅for the
unconditional branch. The sampler retains the same classifier-free combination of score contribu-
tions (Ho & Salimans, 2022) and only replaces the trained conditional score with the analytic PoE
score of the discovered prototypes (Eq. (15)). The only change relative to Liu et al. (2022) is where
the parts come from. Section C provides full details of the selection objective, the closed-form PoE,
and the sampler.
5.2
COMPOSITIONAL BENCHMARK
In test-time compositional generalization, a pretrained diffusion model receives a single OOD query
xq depicting a held-out combination of primitive attributes. The task is to infer from that one query a
distribution over the full held-out class without access to a prebuilt concept library, so reproducing
the query is not enough and the samples must cover other instances of the class. We evaluate on two
benchmarks.
Color-MNIST.
A 32×32 RGB rendering of MNIST with three primitive factors, digit identity (10
values), digit color (4 values), and background color (4 values). The Cartesian product yields 160
compositional slots, of which 120 are seen by the backbone during training and 40 are held out as

6
CONCLUSION, LIMITATIONS, AND FUTURE WORK
We studied test-time compositional generalization by recovering prototypes from a pretrained dif-
fusion model. A prototype is a mode of a noisy marginal pt(xt), reached by ascending the learned
score from a datum and described locally by a Gaussian, with the noise level setting its level of
abstraction. On pretrained CIFAR-10 and CelebA-HQ models, the prototypes of different data are
distinct at fine levels and merge as the level coarsens, while a datum’s own prototype changes sharply
across levels, so a single datum induces a hierarchy of distinct prototypes. Composing the prototypes
discovered from one out-of-distribution query into a product of experts lets the composable diffusion
sampler generate other instances of the query’s held-out class. On ColorMNIST and CelebA, the
generated samples match a near copy of the query on attribute correctness while exceeding it in
diversity, whereas the same sampler conditioned on trained concept tokens lands on neighboring
classes. These findings suggest that diffusion models encode reusable prototypes beyond point
reconstruction or class-level retrieval. The same reading of the score field may extend to other
domains where diffusion models are used, including language modeling (Li et al., 2022; Nie et al.,
2025) and robotic control (Chi et al., 2023).
Limitations. Our protocol follows the standard compositional generalization setting, where primitive
factors are known and test cases are unseen recombinations. The setting remains relevant for
foundation models, which acquire rich primitives during pretraining, so many novel instances are
closer to unseen compositions than to unseen concepts. Human-level intelligence, however, also
learns new primitives and reuses them in later compositions (Fodor & Pylyshyn, 1988; Lake et al.,
2017; 2015). Our framework sometimes discovers novel-looking primitives by interpolating between
known ones (Appendix J). A full mechanism for detecting, consolidating, and reusing genuinely new
primitives remains future work.

PROTOTYPE ANALYSIS DETAILS
The validation of Section 4 uses the released unconditional DDPM checkpoints for CIFAR-10 and
CelebA-HQ (Ho et al., 2020) without any fine-tuning. For each dataset we take a fixed set of held-out
images and the noise-level grid t ∈{10, 20, . . . , 990}. At every (image, level) pair, we forward-noise
the image using Eq. (1) and run the ascent in Eq. (6) with Adam at a learning rate of 0.01 for 200
steps. We swept the ascent length from 50 to 300 steps. The iterate stabilizes by roughly step 200,
and additional steps do not remove further noise from the recovered mode, so all results use 200
steps. The local covariance is a diagonal Laplace estimate at the converged mode, computed using
four Hutchinson probes with finite-difference step 5 × 10−3. We repeat the full procedure for five
ascent seeds. All curves and mode images use the step-200 ascent position, so every divergence pairs
the mode with the covariance measured at that same point.
The separation of Figure 1 is computed between the local Gaussians of two data at the same level.
The five ascent seeds are first pooled into one Gaussian per datum and level by the law of total
variance. Each datum i is then compared with the eight fixed partners i+1, . . . , i+8, taken cyclically
over the held-out set, the per-coordinate symmetric KL is reduced to its median over coordinates, and
the eight values are averaged. The curves show the mean over the held-out data with a ±1 sd band.
Normalized displacement is the Euclidean distance between the unit-normalized prototype locations.
Mode images are shown from a single ascent seed. A second ascent learning rate (0.02) reproduces
every trend on both datasets.

ADDITIONAL EVALUATION DETAILS
Reference sets.
For each OOD class, the reference is a fixed draw of held-out members of the
same class (50 per CelebA class, 10 per ColorMNIST class), sampled once with the query indices
excluded, so no batch is ever compared against its own conditioning image. Every metric is computed
per query batch and aggregated to mean ± standard deviation over the OOD classes.
Why KID rather than FID.
At a batch size of 16, FID’s plug-in estimator is biased upward by
a term that depends on the generator’s own covariance, so small-sample FID can reverse orderings
between methods of different diversity. KID’s U-statistic estimator is unbiased at every sample
size (Bi´nkowski et al., 2018), and we report KID throughout. On CelebA, Inception features separate
face identities far more strongly than face classes, so KID there rewards realistic spread more than
attribute fidelity and we read it as an image-quality measure only.

The diversity score of Section 5.3 is the log determinant of a DPP L-

Under a DPP with kernel L = QSQ, det L is proportional to the probability of drawing the batch as
a set. It is largest when the samples are mutually dissimilar, vanishes when any two coincide, and
the quality factors q2

relevance survives. Higher is better. The distance d is cosine between raw pixel vectors, so no learned

random batch of 16 real members scores −4.7 on ColorMNIST and −5.1 on CelebA, fully spread
but unanchored, since a marginal draw ignores the query.

Backbone training takes roughly 6 hours on a single NVIDIA A40 GPU for ColorMNIST and roughly
50 hours on four NVIDIA RTX 6000 GPUs for CelebA. Test-time prototype discovery and PoE

GENERALIZATION TO UNSEEN PRIMITIVES
Prototype discovery operates on the score field induced by the trained DDPM, so the prototypes it
can surface are confined to regions where that score field is informative. Figure 6 illustrates this
boundary with two queries that share an in-palette pink digit but differ in background. The first places
the digit on a black background, which lies inside the convex hull of the four trained backgrounds
(deep red, navy, dark purple, dark brown), all of which are dark and unsaturated. The second places
the digit on a white background, which falls far outside that hull. The discovered prototypes and the
PoE samples reproduce the held-out black background as a coherent novel primitive, while the white
query collapses onto trained dark backgrounds with the pink digit preserved but the brightness of the
background lost. The discovery procedure therefore inherits the inductive biases of the backbone. It
can compose new primitives that interpolate between seen ones, but it cannot fabricate primitives that
extrapolate beyond the training support.
K
BROADER IMPACTS
A potential positive impact is reducing the data and compute needed to generate novel visual
compositions at test time. A potential negative impact is that the same capability could lower the
barrier to generating face images with novel attribute combinations, which could be misused for
misleading imagery. However, the method operates on small-scale class-conditional DDPMs and
does not approach the fidelity of modern text-to-image systems.
